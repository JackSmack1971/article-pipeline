# Advocate Research Context

**Stream:** Advocate (supporting evidence)
**Thesis:** Cognitive warfare and epistemic warfare are distinct yet mutually reinforcing dimensions of a broader struggle over contested cognition — cognitive warfare targeting individual psychological/neurocognitive processes, epistemic warfare targeting institutional/structural knowledge systems — requiring integration across military studies, political science, STS, philosophy, law, and decolonial scholarship, and a policy shift toward cognitive liberty, epistemic security, and resilient socio-technical/institutional systems.
**Session date:** 2026-08-08 (all sources evaluated against 72-hour freshness protocol where dated within that window)

## Vector 1: Formal doctrine of "cognitive warfare" distinct from information warfare

### Claim ADV-101
- **Statement:** NATO's Allied Command Transformation (ACT) formally defines Cognitive Warfare as activities conducted in synchronization with other Instruments of Power to affect attitudes and behaviours by influencing, protecting, or disrupting individual, group, or population-level cognition to gain advantage over an adversary — a definition distinct from classic information warfare/PSYOPS framing.
- **Source:** NATO Allied Command Transformation, "Allied Command Transformation develops the Cognitive Warfare Concept to Combat Disinformation and Defend Against 'Cognitive Warfare'," act.nato.int, https://www.act.nato.int/article/cogwar-concept/
- **Tier:** T1
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Vector:** 1
- **Strength:** Primary official NATO doctrinal source; directly establishes cognitive warfare as a distinct formal military concept with its own targeting logic (cognition itself) rather than message content.

### Claim ADV-102
- **Statement:** NATO ACT Innovation Hub work states cognitive warfare "is not the means by which to fight; it is the fight itself, where the brain is both the target and the weapon," explicitly distinguishing it from information warfare as a means-to-an-end concept.
- **Source:** NATO's ACT, "Cognitive Warfare: Beyond Military Information Support Operations," https://www.act.nato.int/article/cognitive-warfare-beyond-military-information-support-operations/
- **Tier:** T1
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Vector:** 1
- **Strength:** Primary NATO source explicitly framing the conceptual boundary between cognitive warfare and information/PSYOPS doctrine, supporting the thesis's claim that cognitive warfare is a distinct category.

### Claim ADV-103
- **Statement:** A peer-reviewed conceptual analysis in Frontiers in Big Data (2024) formally analyzes the NATO ACT cognitive warfare "exploratory concept," treating it as an emergent, still-consolidating doctrinal category requiring scholarly clarification — supporting the thesis's call for greater conceptual precision.
- **Source:** Frontiers in Big Data, "Cognitive warfare: a conceptual analysis of the NATO ACT cognitive warfare exploratory concept," 2024, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/big-data/articles/10.3389/fdata.2024.1452129/full (also indexed at PMC: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11565700/)
- **Tier:** T1
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Vector:** 1
- **Strength:** Peer-reviewed academic engagement with NATO's own doctrinal concept, bridging military-studies and academic literature exactly as the thesis calls for.

### Claim ADV-104
- **Statement:** The foundational NATO Innovation Hub paper by Bernard Claverie and François du Cluzel, "The Cognitive Warfare Concept," lays out the doctrinal architecture (targets, effects, domains) underpinning ACT's cognitive warfare concept development, involving contributions from 20+ NATO member nations, commands, academia and industry.
- **Source:** Bernard Claverie & François du Cluzel, "The Cognitive Warfare Concept," Innovation Hub-ACT, https://innovationhub-act.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/CW-article-Claverie-du-Cluzel-final_0.pdf

- **Tier:** T1
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Vector:** 4
- **Strength:** Peer-reviewed legal analysis directly confirming the thesis's claim that existing international humanitarian law inadequately captures cognitive/epistemic harm because of the non-kinetic classification problem.

### Claim ADV-402
- **Statement:** ICRC legal-policy commentary explicitly argues cognitive operations "target the psychological domain without crossing kinetic thresholds, complicating traditional concepts of 'attack,' 'harm,' and 'ruses of war,'" and cautions that "the human brain should not become a battlefield" precisely because existing IHL categories are not built for this threat class.
- **Source:** ICRC, "'Cognitive warfare': why the human brain should not become a battlefield," ICRC Humanitarian Law & Policy blog, February 2026, https://blogs.icrc.org/law-and-policy/2026/02/05/cognitive-warfare-why-the-human-brain-should-not-become-a-battlefield/

## Vector 7: Decolonial/political-science critique of whose knowledge systems are authorized

### Claim ADV-701
- **Statement:** Miranda Fricker's foundational framework identifies two distinct forms of epistemic injustice: testimonial injustice (a speaker's credibility is unjustly deflated due to identity prejudice) and hermeneutical injustice (structural gaps in collective interpretive resources prevent marginalized groups from making sense of and articulating their experiences) — establishing that whose knowledge counts as valid is itself a site of structural power contestation.
- **Source:** Miranda Fricker, "Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing," reviewed at Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews, https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/epistemic-injustice-power-and-the-ethics-of-knowing/ ; overview at Wikipedia (secondary), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_injustice
- **Tier:** T1 (primary monograph, reviewed in academic journal) / T3 (Wikipedia overview, used only as convenience cross-reference)
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Vector:** 7
- **Strength:** Directly supplies the philosophical vocabulary the thesis needs for its claim that epistemic warfare operates through institutions and authorities that validate knowledge — Fricker's framework demonstrates this validation process is inherently contestable and unevenly distributed by power.

### Claim ADV-702
- **Statement:** Cambridge University's Episteme journal has published peer-reviewed scholarship critically examining "whether Frickerian accounts of epistemic justice can promote decoloniality," engaging directly with the limits and potential of mainstream analytic epistemic-injustice theory for addressing colonial and Global-South knowledge-system delegitimization.
- **Source:** Cambridge Core, "Can Frickerian Accounts of Epistemic Justice Promote Decoloniality?: A Critical Examination," Episteme, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/episteme/article/can-frickerian-accounts-of-epistemic-justice-promote-decoloniality-a-critical-examination/9EC61243CEFD835AA9C166D89BCBA3E1
- **Tier:** T1
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Vector:** 7
- **Strength:** Peer-reviewed philosophy journal directly engaging decolonial critique of epistemic-injustice theory, substantiating the thesis's explicit integration of "decolonial scholarship" into the analysis of whose knowledge systems get authorized in epistemic contestation.

### Claim ADV-703
- **Statement:** Scholarship notes that Fricker's original testimonial/hermeneutical injustice framework was developed with primary reference to epistemic injustice experienced by oppressed identity groups within the Global North, prompting subsequent scholarly efforts to extend or adapt the framework to address broader, transnational decolonial concerns about whose knowledge infrastructures are treated as authoritative.
- **Source:** Cambridge Core Episteme article (as above), https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/episteme/article/can-frickerian-accounts-of-epistemic-justice-promote-decoloniality-a-critical-examination/9EC61243CEFD835AA9C166D89BCBA3E1

- **Vector:** 7
